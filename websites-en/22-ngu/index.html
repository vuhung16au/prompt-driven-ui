01. THESIS

Narrow houses are not miniaturized villas

CORE THESIS

Urban tube houses typically measure only 3m to 4m across, with depth 4 to 5 times greater, hemmed in by three adjacent shared party walls.

The most common architectural blunder is attempting to shrink down a villa or large apartment layout into a tight footprint. The outcome is suffocating darkness, pitch-black corridors, and bulky concrete staircases strangling living areas.

A
Light as Structural Material: Puncturing intermediate slabs to establish a rooftop lightwell rather than relying 100% on electric lamps during the day.
B
Perimeter Steel Stair: Removing heavy central masonry staircases to unlock continuous horizontal flow and visual permeability.

03. CONDITIONS & DECISIONS

Confronting Two Core Constraints

CORE PROBLEM

How do we prevent a deep 3.2m tube house from becoming a dark, oppressive tunnel?

CONDITION 01

Severe Lack of Natural Daylight

Flanked on three sides by neighbor walls. By day, ground floor and core areas require 100% artificial illumination with persistent stagnant humidity.

CONDITION 02

Cramped Corridors & Obstructive Stairs

A massive concrete staircase planted dead-center consumes over 30% of the cross-section width, fracturing the house into dark fragmented cubicles.

DECISION 01 Liberating the Micro-Climate Lightwell

Carving an aperture through 2nd and 3rd floor slabs to create a 6.5m² vertical atrium beneath a glazed roof. Angled timber louvers soften daylight down to the ground floor living area.

DECISION 02 Replacing Masonry with Perimeter Steel Stairs

Demolishing the central concrete stairs and deploying a lightweight steel stringer with open oak treads hugs the western boundary wall, recovering 35% of usable floor area.
