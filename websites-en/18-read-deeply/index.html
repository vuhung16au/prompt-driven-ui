Curriculum

Four Steps to Dissecting Arguments

Differentiate between what authors actually ask and what you assume they are asking. Identify implicit assumptions in how problems are framed.

Outcome: You will no longer go off-topic when beginning to discuss complex texts.

Peel back long paragraphs into core propositions. Uncover the hidden thesis statement among convoluted explanations.

Outcome: See through to the skeletal structure of any profound written work.

Evaluate the weight of presented evidence. Differentiate between objective facts, expert opinions, and logical fallacies.

Outcome: Resist being easily persuaded by beautifully written but weak arguments.

Organize ideas to reply systematically. Learn to partially agree, partially rebut, and offer fresh perspectives instead of mere criticism.

Outcome: Write sharp, composed, and highly constructive counterarguments.

Sample Lesson

Read the following excerpt and select the sentence containing the main thesis.

I

n an era of overwhelming information, many believe that reading as many books as possible is the ideal. They set goals of a book a week, proud of their rapid consumption of text.

Speed-reading often leads to vague retention, superficial understanding, and quick forgetting when new information overrides the old. This skill requires intentional slowness, a rarity in today's rushed pace of life.

Click on a sentence in the paragraph to test whether it is the thesis statement.

Analysis

This sentence is a transitional statement. It pushes back against the habit of speed-reading mentioned previously, but it does not yet provide the core solution or the author's comprehensive perspective on reading.

Correct

This is indeed the thesis statement. It clearly defines "the capacity for deep reading" and asserts its paramount importance ("the definitive skill for intellectual maturity"), encompassing the entire meaning of the excerpt.

Instructor

Discussion Moderator

The moderator does not stand at a podium to force-feed knowledge. Their role is akin to a diligent librarian: asking piercing questions, pointing out blind spots in your arguments, and keeping the discussion strictly on course.

  • On-the-spot logical text analysis.
  • Constructive debate, no ad hominem attacks.
  • Extensive experience in academic group moderation.
